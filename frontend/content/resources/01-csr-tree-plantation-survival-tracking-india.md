# CSR Tree Plantation in India: How to Prove Survival (Not Just Planting)

Planting day makes a great photo. Boards and auditors ask a harder question months later: **did the trees survive?**

For Indian corporates and the agencies that run their CSR events, impact now means **geo-tagged registration, survival monitoring, and a clear plantation report** — not a WhatsApp album. Schedule VII spending is scrutinised more closely every year, and sustainability teams are expected to show that plantation budgets translated into living cover on the ground, not just saplings in a nursery invoice.

This guide explains what credible CSR plantation evidence looks like in India, how survival tracking fits into BRSR and internal ESG workflows, and where agencies can productise a repeatable **Plant → Track → Report** offer for their brand clients.

## Why photo-only CSR plantations fail reviews

Most CSR plantation drives share the same failure pattern:

- Trees are counted once on event day, then forgotten  
- Locations are approximate, duplicated, or missing entirely  
- Survival after 6–12 months is unknown or anecdotal  
- ESG and BRSR teams cannot defend numbers in front of audit committees  

CAG-style audits of large plantation programmes keep finding the same gap: **proof, not planting**, is where programmes break. A district officer, NGO partner, or internal reviewer can accept a ceremonial grove in year one. By year three, the question shifts to mortality rates, species mix, maintenance spend, and whether the claimed hectares still match satellite or field reality.

Photo-only programmes also struggle when leadership changes. The sustainability manager who ran the event moves on; the NGO contact rotates; the Excel sheet lives on one laptop. Without a digital registry tied to GPS and time-stamped photos, reconstruction is expensive and often inconclusive.

## What “good” CSR plantation evidence looks like

Credible CSR plantation MRV in India usually includes five layers:

1. **GPS + photos at planting** for every sapling, plot, or belt segment  
2. **Species and programme tag** (CSR event, campus, community, vendor block)  
3. **Survival surveys** at set intervals (for example 3, 6, and 12 months)  
4. **Optional satellite health checks** on the mapped work area  
5. **A scheduled report** your brand or client can file with internal audit or external assurance  

None of this replaces statutory forest approvals or issues registry carbon credits. Aranyix and similar MRV platforms help teams **measure, monitor, and export audit-prep evidence**. Final BRSR assurance remains with your company and appointed assurance provider.

Good evidence is also **append-only**: survival checks add new records rather than overwriting planting-day data. That timeline matters when someone asks whether a site was replanted after a drought or whether numbers were revised quietly.

## Geo-tagged tree plantation for CSR — practical checklist

Use this checklist before your next CSR plantation weekend:

- [ ] Site or polygon mapped before the event (not sketched from memory afterward)  
- [ ] Field app captures GPS accuracy, species, and required photos per plant or plot  
- [ ] Each tree or plot gets a digital ID or QR passport linked to the CSR project  
- [ ] Care plan assigned (1, 2, or 3 years if your programme includes maintenance)  
- [ ] First survival check scheduled before crews leave the site  
- [ ] Export template agreed with your ESG or audit contact (BRSR Principle 6, internal pack, or client report)  

Geo-tagging alone is not sufficient. It is the anchor that makes survival tracking and scheduled reporting possible. Without coordinates, you cannot reconcile field counts with satellite stress maps or prove that the same physical trees were revisited months later.

## Survival tracking cadence that boards understand

Survival tracking does not need to be daily. It needs to be **predictable** and **documented**:

| Interval | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 0–30 days | Establish baseline registry; fix mis-tags and missing photos |
| 3 months | First mortality signal after initial establishment |
| 6 months | Monsoon stress check in most Indian climates |
| 12 months | Annual CSR / ESG reporting anchor |

Field teams should record whether each tagged unit is alive, stressed, dead, or replaced — with a photo when status changes. Programme managers roll those results into survival percentages by project, vendor, or state. That is the number your board slide should show alongside spend and hectares.

Optional satellite NDVI or SAR integrity layers help prioritise where to send crews, especially on large community blocks. Satellite signals guide field work; they do not replace ground truth for audit conversations.

## Reporting without over-claiming

CSR reports should separate facts from estimates:

- **Facts:** geo-tags, photos, survival counts, maintenance visits  
- **Estimates:** modeled CO₂e or canopy cover projections, clearly labeled as audit-prep or indicative MRV — not certified issuance  

If your client asks for “carbon impact,” explain that CSR events rarely map to registry credits without a separate methodology and external process. Honest language protects both the agency and the brand during assurance.

## FAQ

**Is geo-tagging enough for BRSR?**  
Geo-tags are the start. Pair them with survival data, species records, and a consistent export your assurance team recognises. BRSR Principle 6 reviewers increasingly expect traceability, not aggregate tree counts.

**Do we need carbon credits language?**  
Usually no for CSR events. If you estimate CO₂e, label it as a **modelled estimate / audit preparation** — not certified issuance from Verra, Gold Standard, or other registries.

**Can small CSR events skip digital MRV?**  
Small events can start with a single mapped plot and 50–100 tagged trees. The discipline matters more than scale: one clean registry beats ten thousand approximate pins.

**Who should own the registry — corporate or NGO?**  
Define this in the MOU. Corporates often want read-only dashboard access while NGOs or implementation partners capture field data. Role-based access avoids disputes at reporting time.

**How long should records be retained?**  
Align with your client’s audit cycle. Many corporates retain plantation evidence for 3–7 years. Append-only history simplifies later reconstruction.
