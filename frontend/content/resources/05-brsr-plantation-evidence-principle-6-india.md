# BRSR Plantation Evidence in India: What Principle 6 Reviewers Actually Look For

SEBI's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) puts environmental performance on the board agenda. For companies that fund tree plantation through CSR or operations greening, **Principle 6** is where plantation claims meet assurance. The weak pattern is familiar: a headline tree count in the annual report, thin backup in the working papers, and a scramble when the assurance provider asks *where* those trees are and *whether they still live*.

This guide is for sustainability, ESG, and CSR leads at Indian corporates — and for the agencies that deliver their plantation days. It explains what **BRSR plantation evidence** usually means in practice, how to structure Plant → Track → Report without over-claiming carbon credits, and how to keep a clean trail for internal audit and external assurance.

## Principle 6 is about performance, not ceremony

BRSR Principle 6 covers how an entity manages environmental impacts. Plantation programmes show up as:

- Hectares or trees planted under CSR Schedule VII activities  
- Operational green belts (factories, mines, campuses, corridors)  
- Supplier or community programmes that the company funds and discloses  

Assurance providers do not need a romantic planting-day story. They need traceable claims: what was planted, where, by whom, under which programme code, and what follow-up happened. A photo album and a vendor invoice are not enough when numbers appear in the BRSR annexures.

## The evidence stack that survives review

Treat plantation disclosure like any other quantitative ESG claim. Build a stack:

1. **Registry** — each tree, plot, or belt segment has an ID, GPS, species, date, and project tag  
2. **Source documents** — work orders, NGO / agency MoUs, nursery invoices linked to the same project ID  
3. **Survival / condition** — timed field checks (for example 3 / 6 / 12 months) with status and photo when status changes  
4. **Spatial context** — mapped polygons for belts and community blocks so counts can be reconciled to area  
5. **Export pack** — a repeatable PDF / XLSX that matches how your assurance team wants Principle 6 working papers  

Optional satellite NDVI or SAR integrity layers help programme managers prioritise field visits. They guide evidence collection; they do not replace ground truth for BRSR working papers.

## What usually fails BRSR plantation claims

| Failure | Why it hurts assurance |
|---|---|
| Aggregate "trees planted" with no locations | Cannot sample or verify |
| Duplicate or missing GPS | Inflated or untestable counts |
| No survival after monsoon | Year-2 disclosure contradicts year-1 optimism |
| Carbon tonnes stated as "credits" | Over-claim; registries and methodologies are separate |
| Vendor Excel only on one laptop | Not an entity-controlled record |

CAG-style audits of public plantation programmes have shown the same pattern for years: planting is easy to announce; survival and location are hard to prove. Listed companies face a different regulator, but the evidence logic is the same.

## Mapping plantation data to BRSR language

You do not need to invent a new language for auditors. Map fields once:

- **Activity description** → CSR project name / EC green-belt condition / campus greening code  
- **Quantitative indicator** → trees registered, hectares mapped, survival % at last survey  
- **Methodology note** → how GPS accuracy, mortality, and replacements are defined  
- **Limitations** → sites not yet resurveyed; modeled CO₂e labeled as estimate only  

Keep facts and estimates in separate columns. Facts: geo-tags, photos, survival counts, maintenance visits. Estimates: modeled biomass or CO₂e. If you mention VM0047, ISO 14064-2, or similar frameworks, state clearly that your stack is audit preparation / structured evidence, not registry issuance or legal certification.

## A 90-day BRSR plantation readiness pilot

Corporates that are starting from photo-only programmes do not need a multi-year overhaul before the next filing. A focused pilot works:

**Days 1–15 — Scope**  
Pick 1–2 live plantation projects (one CSR event block, one operational green belt). Agree the Principle 6 lines you will support this year.

**Days 16–45 — Register**  
Map polygons, register trees or plots with GPS + photos, tag species and programme, freeze a baseline export.

**Days 46–75 — First survival pass**  
Run a structured check; record alive / stressed / dead / replaced; attach photos on status change.

**Days 76–90 — Assurance pack**  
Export the working paper your assurance provider asked for. Document definitions and known gaps. Decide the cadence for the next financial year.

This is the same Plant → Track → Report loop mining and CSR teams use for operational greening — applied to disclosure discipline.

## Roles: who owns what

- **ESG / sustainability** — disclosure language, assurance liaison, methodology notes  
- **CSR / plant ops** — field vendors, agencies, nursery quality  
- **NGO / event agency** — day-of capture; should not be the only copy of the registry  
- **Internal audit / assurance** — sample selection, evidence completeness  

Put registry ownership in the MoU. Corporates should keep read access and export rights even when partners capture field data.

## FAQ

**Is a tree count enough for Principle 6?**  
Rarely. Pair counts with locations, programme tags, and at least one survival snapshot in the reporting period when you claim living cover.

**Do we need carbon credit language in BRSR?**  
Usually no. If you disclose modeled removals, label them as estimates and keep them separate from certified credits.

**Can agencies hold the only copy of plantation data?**  
They can capture it. You should retain exportable, entity-accessible records for assurance.

**How often should we resurvey?**  
Match monsoon and filing calendars. Many teams use 3 / 6 / 12 month anchors for CSR blocks and denser checks on regulatory green belts.

**What if last year's numbers were photo-only?**  
Disclose limitations honestly, start a digital registry for new planting, and avoid restating unverifiable historical pins as GPS truth.
