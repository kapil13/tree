## The buyer problem: planting is easy; proof is hard

Most Indian plantation programmes fail the same way. Trees are counted on event day. GPS is missing, duplicated, or sketched later. Survival after monsoon is unknown. When BRSR Principle 6, internal audit, or a mining EC green-belt review asks for sampleable evidence, teams rebuild from invoices and photos.

That gap is why buyers search for **plantation monitoring software CSR** and **geo-tagged plantation MRV**. They need a **tree plantation MRV platform** that turns field work into entity-controlled records — not another headline tree count.

CAG-style public audits and private assurance share one lesson: **announce planting freely; prove survival and location carefully**. Software that promises certification or credit issuance without a separate methodology is selling the wrong outcome.

## Plant — register every tree or plot as evidence

**Plant** means registration discipline, not only digging holes.

1. Map the work area (polygon or belt segment) before or on planting day.  
2. Capture GPS, species, photos, and programme tag (CSR event, EC green belt, community block, campus).  
3. Assign a digital ID / passport so resurveys attach to the same physical unit.  
4. Link nursery or vendor documents to the same project code.

Offline Android field apps matter in remote mine belts and rural CSR sites; web dashboards matter for managers who never walk the plot. Without clean Plant data, Track and Report become fiction.

## Track — survival, stress, and re-planting on a cadence

**Track** is the difference between a ceremony and a programme.

| Interval | Typical purpose |
|---|---|
| 0–30 days | Fix mis-tags; freeze baseline registry |
| 3 months | First mortality signal |
| 6 months | Monsoon / drought stress check |
| 12 months | Annual CSR / ESG / EC reporting anchor |

Field crews record alive / stressed / dead / replaced — with a photo when status changes. Managers roll survival % by project, vendor, state, or mine lease. Optional satellite NDVI + SAR layers help prioritise large hectares; they **guide** visits and do not replace ground truth for audit packs.

## Report — exports that survive sampling

**Report** means a repeatable PDF / XLSX pack your assurance, EC, or CSR committee recognises:

- Tree / plot IDs, GPS, species, planting date, programme code  
- Survival snapshot for the reporting window  
- Methodology notes (GPS accuracy, mortality definitions, replacements)  
- Limitations (sites not yet resurveyed; modeled CO₂e labeled as estimate only)

Map the same fields to [BRSR Principle 6 plantation evidence](/resources/brsr-plantation-evidence-principle-6-india), or to EC green-belt and CAMPA-adjacent working papers. For CAMPA-style programmes, treat digital MRV as a **complement** to statutory systems such as e-Green Watch — not a replacement. See [CAMPA afforestation](/solutions/campa-afforestation) and [BRSR / ESG](/solutions/brsr-esg).

Oil and construction industrial greening can reuse the same stack when belts or campuses need evidence discipline — keep them adjacent, not the headline use case.

## FAQ

**What should plantation MRV software in India actually do?**  
Register geo-tagged trees or plots, support survival resurveys, fuse optional satellite stress signals, and export packs auditors or EC reviewers can sample. Marketing claims without field IDs are not MRV.

**Is digital MRV the same as carbon credit issuance?**  
No. MRV assembles measurement and monitoring evidence. Credit issuance needs separate methodologies, validators, and registries (for example Verra or Gold Standard). Keep those paths distinct in slides and MoUs.

**Can Aranyix replace e-Green Watch or statutory forest systems?**  
No. For CAMPA-adjacent work, use Aranyix as complementary programme MRV and audit-prep evidence alongside official systems — never as a substitute for mandated portals.

**Do CSR agencies need their own MRV stack?**  
Agencies can capture field data, but brands should retain exportable, entity-accessible records. White-label packs work when registry ownership and report cadence are written into the MoU.

**How big should a first pilot be?**  
Prefer one or two live projects (for example one CSR block and one operational green belt) over a nationwide roll-out. Prove Plant → Track → Report in 90 days, then expand.

**Where do oil or construction greening programmes fit?**  
Same evidence stack when industrial belts or campuses need geo-tags and survival — treat them as adjacent use cases after CSR, mining, BRSR, and CAMPA-complement priorities.
